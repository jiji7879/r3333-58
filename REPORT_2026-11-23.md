# The 2026-11-23 swarms: one consolidated report

Three Ultramath swarms ran today against the `R(3,3,3,3) ≤ 58` campaign — `smwsyh6tk` (cross-arm),
`smwswz4x9` (zero-set multiplicity) and `smwtqk6ku` (the 19-spoke bound) — together with five
bundles supplied by the person. This is the single document that says what came out of all of it,
what was verified independently, and what is still open.

**Bottom line: `R_4(3) ∈ [51, 62]` has not moved.** 31,751 of the 113,179 frozen cover rows are OPEN,
all 381 rows of the `(15,14,14,14)` profile are OPEN, no colouring or refutation of a wall row was
produced, and no certificate exists for any row of that profile. What did change is that four layers
of the campaign are now decided, several dead ends are closed with proofs rather than impressions,
and seven errors in the record or in the shipped packages were found and fixed.

---

## 1. Where the campaign stands

- `51 ≤ R_4(3) ≤ 62`. Lower bound Chung 1973 (Zbl 0258.05111); upper bound Fettes–Kramer–
  Radziszowski 2004 (Zbl 1075.05057), both read off Radziszowski's survey DS1 rev. #18 p. 56. The
  survey warns that **Chung's printed matrix has a typo** — a triangle `(31, 7, 28)` in colour 1.
- The frozen cover is complete: `K58.Replay.frozenCover`, unconditional, 113,179 rows.
- The kernel audits 367 declarations in `lean/K58/Check.lean`, all axioms inside
  `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.
- The wall: the seven small profiles, 11,585 rows, no row of which has ever been closed by any
  method tried. The `(15,14,14,14)` profile is the smallest of them, 381 rows.

## 4. The 19-spoke bound (`smwtqk6ku`) — real, and five ways narrower than hoped

On two rows the bundle proves any extension needs `U+V ≥ 19` (colour-1 edges from the root's 15-arm
to two marked pentagons). **The four-stage proof replays here in 18 min 18 s with no proof archives**,
by regenerating every certificate. Then the swarm attacked the obvious question — can it be
generalised cheaply? — and answered it in five independent ways, all negative:

| # | finding | verified here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the configuration is on **159 rows, not 169** (ten have the mark inside A); all 159 cores are **pairwise non-isomorphic**, so isomorphism saves 3.6 % and nothing on a relevant row | **yes** — 9,211 VF2 tests on the coloured cores, 0 isomorphic pairs |
| 2 | **the 26-vertex core carries none of the bound**: every row admits `U+V ≤ 18` on it, so no core-level certificate can give 19; the content is on 35–44 vertex arm boards (1 s vs 270 s) | **yes** — five witnesses re-checked from the definition |
| 3 | **no linear or counting certificate at all**: fractional points satisfying totalness, triangle constraints and pins attain `U+V ≤ 89/12`, with `19/4` and `11/2` on the two published rows and `0` with pins dropped | **yes** — four rows re-verified in exact integer arithmetic |
| 4 | **the four rectangles do not transfer**: on the bundle's own `(14,5)` board, `U ≤ 6` is satisfiable on two rows (`U=6, V=18` and `U=6, V=19`), and `(U≤8,V≤10)` is satisfiable under the pins all 159 rows share | **yes** — both counterexamples and the shared-pin witness verified from the definition |
| 5 | the row content is the **150 spoke pins**: the other 175 core pins take 6 values over the 159 rows, the spokes 145, and no spoke is ever pinned to colour 1; sharing skeletons saves ~1.5 %, so ~54,000 certificates is not an artefact | **yes** — recomputed over the bundle's rows |

Two further boundaries: **34 vertices do not suffice** (`U+V=18` attained on `(13,5)` for 88 of 169
rows — verified), and **no board of 36–39 vertices can force 20** (all five splits at 19 are attained
on such boards — all 14 witnesses verified). So a strengthening to 20 needs boards larger than 39
vertices.

Also measured, and negative: at 50,000 conflicts the bound **does not shorten the search** (paired
ratios 0.93, 1.58, 1.13, 0.94, every run at the limit).

The eighth member closed the board hierarchy: 26 vertices admit `U ≤ 6` on 165 rows, 30 vertices on
all 169, 34 admit `U+V = 18` on 88, and only at 35 does anything stand — so every board below the
two-arm interface is dead for this purpose, and the cheap route is necessarily a 35-vertex question.

## 4b. The upper bound on `U+V` (`smwu1x5uy`) — exact, attained, and useless for closure

Three swarms were not enough; a fourth asked whether `U+V` can be bounded *above*, since a ceiling
below the floor would close a row outright.

**On branch 62 the range is exactly `[21, 40]`, and every number in it is checked:**

| | value | how |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| floor | **21** | short-spoke chain, DRAT replayed |
| cells at 21 | **4 of 22** | 18 excluded; any 21-colouring has `min(U,V) >= 9` |
| ceiling | **40** | hand proof (`alpha(G[F]) = 4`), 58-vertex instance (proof reproduced byte-identically), 44-vertex certificate |
| attained at | 35, 36, 37 vertices | witnesses checked from the definition |
| dead by | 44 vertices | no known top colouring reaches `(14,14)` |
| row max | **40** | instance fact at 58 vertices |

Four independent members derived the same ceiling, and the two errors found in the process were mine:
the trivial cap is **50**, not the 75 I wrote (the pentagon side binds, five A-neighbours per pentagon
vertex), and the campaign's 36 sits four below the true board maximum.

**Why it closes nothing.** Closure needs an upper bound of 20 against a floor of 21. The ceiling is
40 and it is *attained at board level*, so no argument confined to any board can produce a
contradiction — proved, not searched. The board-level route is dead in both directions on all five
rows, whose board maxima are 32, 33, 35, 38 and 40 against floors of 19, 19, 19, 19 and 21.

**The one experiment with no neutral outcome, and its result.** An extension of branch 62 must have
`U+V = k` for some `k` in `[21,40]`, so deciding all twenty decides the row: all-UNSAT closes it,
any-SAT gives a `K_58` colouring and hence `R_4(3) >= 59`. The judge built all twenty instances at 58
vertices and ran them at 320 s and 10 M conflicts each — **all twenty OPEN**, 1 h 47 m of compute.
Pinning the total does not make the row tractable at that budget; the instances are as hard as the row.

## 5. The measurements that came out of the compute

- **Four one-hour runs** (one case per wall profile, 3,600 s and 200 M conflicts): **0 of 4 closed.**
  Each used ~1,500 CPU-s of 3,600 s wall (41.6 %) because four cases and two swarms shared four
  cores. The proof stream reached 3.0 / 3.1 / 3.4 / **5.6 GB** at 45 minutes.
- **One two-hour run**, full core, same instance: **UNKNOWN at 170,229,433 conflicts** (22,114/s,
  959 M decisions), 45 GB of DRAT, rate flattening at ~22 k/s. That is 425× the first-pass budget
  and it closes nothing.
- **The certificate stream costs 21 GB per search-hour.** A 24 h run on one wall row would need
  ~500 GB of proof. The campaign's design (retain the proof, check it afterwards) cannot support
  hour-scale runs on the wall; the checker cap of 300 s would also discard a 5 GB proof.
- Two instrument defects: the harness hard-codes `--quiet`, so **no conflict count is ever recorded**;
  and `proof_bytes` records retained rather than emitted bytes.

## 8. What is open

1. **The structural lemma** — the campaign's critical path, still unwritten: no vertex of a
   triangle-free 4-colouring of K₅₈ has two colour-degrees ≤ 14.
2. **31,751 cover rows**, of which the seven small profiles (11,585) are the wall.
3. **The one live question about the 19-spoke bound**: does a 35-vertex `(14,5)` board force
   `U+V ≥ 19` on a row that carries it? If yes, ≤134 instances give the bound instead of ~54,000
   certificates. Every hard instance tried resisted 45–300 s. m3's counterexample does not bear on it
   (its witness has `U+V = 24`).
4. **Attainability of 19 and any strengthening to 20** — open; 19 is attained on 36–39 vertex boards,
   so 20 needs larger.
5. **The encoding-adequacy bridge** (`review/LEAN_ENCODING_ADEQUACY.md`): `satisfiable_atLeast` is
   proved; guarded constraints, the support-selector family, assembly and the hash binding remain.

## 10. The honest summary

Four layers are now decided and each is verified well enough to be relied on: the frozen cover, the
cross-arm template test (three ways), the zero-set multiplicity census (down to 33 arm-level
profiles), and the local structure of the 19-spoke bound on two rows. Seven errors were caught, five
of them in our own records.

None of that closes a row. Every certified layer so far has arrived, been verified, and left its rows
open — and the measurements say the wall is not a matter of budget: 170 M conflicts in two hours with
a 45 GB certificate bought nothing. The campaign's own conclusion has not changed: the margin is the
structural lemma, and no amount of the work above substitutes for it.
