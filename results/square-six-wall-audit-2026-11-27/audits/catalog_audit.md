## 1. What `catalog.py` actually proves

Source: `.scratch/square-six-v1/src/K58_square_six/catalog.py`.

For a template matrix, a pair of colors $a,b$, and a labeled support $S$, define abstract two-color extendability as existence of an outside-spoke assignment $\sigma:S\to\{a,b\}$ with no monochromatic triangle using an internal edge of $S$. This is equivalent to writing

$$S=I_a\cup I_b,$$

where $I_a$ is independent in the color-$a$ graph and $I_b$ is independent in the color-$b$ graph. Vertices in the intersection may receive either color.

- `maximal_independent` constructs all independent sets by adding the least set bit to an already indexed subset, then tests maximality against every outside vertex. Restricting independent sets to $S$ and extending them to maximal independent sets proves that unions of **maximal** independent sets suffice for the global yes/no test.
- Initially `ok[ma|mb]` is set for maximal-independent-set unions. `spread(..., downward=True)` then makes `ok[S]` true exactly when $S$ is contained in such a union. Thus `ok` is hereditary, as required by the abstract spoke semantics.
- `factored_masks(matrix,a,b)` constructs unions of two same-orientation red-red-blue triangles whose tips are joined blue. It runs both color-role orders. Overlap of the two outer pairs gives union size four, five, or six. These are precisely the README's local two-triangle ingredients, not arbitrary graph motifs.
- `spread(excluded, downward=False)` marks every superset of such a union as non-extendable. The check `ok[mask] + excluded[mask] == 1` for every mask is therefore an exhaustive finite proof, for each encoded matrix and pair, that every abstractly non-extendable support contains a generated two-triangle union and that no generated union is falsely excluded.
- A minimal non-extendable support must itself occur among `factors`: otherwise a proper contained factor would already exclude it. Testing all one-vertex deletions suffices for all proper subsets because `ok` is hereditary. Complementarity also ensures that each tested factor is itself non-extendable. The size census and the rejection of sizes outside 4/5/6 follow.

This all-subsets comparison is more than selection or scoring, but only for the abstract question and the exact matrices supplied. It is not a theorem for arbitrary rows, arbitrary templates, or every physical node on the wall.

### Physical-parent qualification

Catalog extendability does not know the outside vertex's identity or its pinned spoke colors. On an actual parent, the allowed assignments can only be more restricted:

- abstract non-extendability still yields a valid local obstruction;
- abstract extendability does **not** imply extendability with a particular physical outside vertex, global parent satisfiability, or an unresolved unit-propagation node;
- “minimal” is template-relative, not necessarily minimal after that outside vertex's pins and other parent constraints.

The selected direct and short clauses therefore still need their separate original-parent RUP/DRAT obligations. Catalog equality is not a proof-prefix substitute.

`general.py` is a different construction: it enumerates individual conditional-triangle clauses in chosen blocks, simplifies using units, filters for additional propagation, checks candidates, sorts by length/literals, and stops at its limit. It does not reproduce the fixed branch-44 selection order. The two-triangle support generator in `catalog.py` must not be conflated with this short-clause generator.

Only 16T, 16U, and 15T are actual arms in `study.json`. `verify_catalog` creates 15U syntactically as the upper-left 15-by-15 induced matrix of 16U; with the recorded vertex list, this deletes vertex 32. It is a comparison template, not a fourth physical arm.

For each color, each full 16-arm matrix also passed a direct SRG(16,5,0,2) parameter check: five-regular, adjacent vertices have zero common neighbors, and nonadjacent vertices have two. This checks the package's Clebsch parameters without invoking a whole-wall result.

### Checker omission, but not a numeric error

In the pair loop for `(a,b)`, `catalog.py` sets `six = rims[a]`; it does not test `rims[b]` there. Consequently the largest color's rim support/extendability counts are never checked by the packaged routine. The supplemental bounded audit computed both rim colors for every pair. Every missing cell also matched the README, including total rims 320/200 and extendability 128/80/50 as appropriate. Thus the table is numerically supported by this audit, but `verify_catalog` alone does not establish every cell under a “rim of either chosen color” reading.

The rim code stores six-vertex **support bitmasks in a set**. Its path conditions fix the smallest start vertex and one orientation, and the set collapses multiple cycles on the same support. The counts are therefore supports admitting at least one monochromatic simple six-cycle, not numbers of cycles. All chords are still used when `ok` tests spoke extendability.

Do not conflate:

- the 32 (16T) or 20 (15T) minimal six-site paired-triangle obstructions, none of which has a monochromatic rim in any template color;
- the 320 or 200 supports carrying a monochromatic rim, and the subsets of those that admit the chosen spoke pair;
- the separate conditional lower bound of 22 marked monochromatic hexagons.

## 3. What is selection/scoring rather than exhaustive coverage

Sources: `.scratch/square-six-v1/src/K58_square_six/evidence/study.json` and `wedges.json`.

`study.json` stores only candidate totals `{'4': 8546, '6': 4736}` and `cap: 192`; it does not store the candidate lists or the generation/scoring code. `catalog.py` does not reconstruct those totals or the selected records, and source search found no consumer that validates the stored `counts`/`shapes` metadata. In particular, the 16-of-80 and 11-of-55 complementary-pentagon subcounts are internally consistent with the stored totals but are not independently rechecked by `verify_catalog`.

The retained direct set has 384 distinct serialized goals: 192 square and 192 paired-triangle records. Its actual distribution is:

- square: 6 from 16T, 54 from 16U, and 132 from 15T;
- paired triangles: all 192 from 15T, with only 16 distinct outside vertices, namely 38–46 and 51–57;
- no retained six-site direct cut comes from 16T or 16U.

This is not a claim that other candidates are invalid or useless. It demonstrates that benchmark selection cannot be reported as globally exhaustive physical-node coverage. The stored unresolved/propagation tests concern selected instances only.

The retained short set contains 1,020 distinct clauses even after sorting literals: widths 3/4/5 occur 364/306/350 times. The stock list has 189 distinct clauses. `wedges.json` also contains scalar metadata `limbs=768`, `all_short=1284`, and `extra_short=364`, but only `clauses` is the serialized retained list. Those scalars do not independently regenerate the selection; in particular, witness occurrences need not denote disjoint new clauses, so simple arithmetic on the scalar fields is not an audit of the final 1,020.
