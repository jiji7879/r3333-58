**So: the lower bound 51 is Chung 1973, and the upper bound 62 is Fettes–Kramer–Radziszowski
2004.** Both attributions were previously carried in the record from memory; they are now read
off a primary source. The zbMATH record for Chung's paper has *no* review or summary, so the
number 51 is not quotable from zbMATH itself — only from the survey. The Fettes–Kramer–
Radziszowski review (Ralph Faudree, zbMATH) also says "The best lower bound is 51", which is a
second, independent statement of the same pair.

### 1.1 The Chung typo — an operational warning

The survey states that Chung's displayed 51-colouring matrix contains a **typo**: the
triangle `(31, 7, 28)` appears in colour 1. The construction is correct; the printed matrix is
not. Anyone reconstructing Chung's K₅₁ colouring from the 1973 paper and checking it will find
monochromatic triangles and may wrongly conclude the bound fails. If this project ever
transcribes that matrix — for near-miss analysis of the K₅₁ case — the entry must be corrected,
and the check `tools/verify_coloring.py` is what decides it.

### 1.2 The closed construction families are the survey's own statement

The sentence above ("neither by some other group partition means [Ana2]") is the survey's
statement that the group-partition/Cayley route cannot improve 51. This matches the ruling
relayed into every swarm since `smwp3xc7p`; it is now citable as DS1 rev. #18 p. 56 + [Ana2],
not as an unsourced recall.

## 2. The November 2026 preprint (≤ 60)

- arXiv:[2610.36648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36648), Milos Tatarevic, *An upper bound of 60
  for the Ramsey number R(3,3,3,3)*. Metadata read from the abstract page on 2026-11-23:
  **v1 submitted 27 Oct 2026, v2 revised 6 Nov 2026**; the comment field reads
  "15 pages, 3 figures. **This revision improves the upper bound from 61 to 60**".
  Abstract: "We improve the upper bound on the classical Ramsey number $R(3,3,3,3)$ from 62 to
  60. The proof is based on a global incidence count, classifications of small neighborhoods and
  exhaustive SAT computations. We formalize the mathematical reductions in Lean and refute every
  resulting formula with two SAT solvers."
- zbMATH record: zbMATH id [903229195](https://zbmath.org/903229195), arXiv database, MSC 05C55
  (primary) / 05D10, licence non-exclusive-distrib/1.0. **No Zbl number.** The summary is
  withheld ("contents unavailable due to conflicting licenses"), so nothing beyond the title is
  available from zbMATH.
- OEIS [A003323](https://oeis.org/A003323) carries the line "Tatarevic (2026, Theorem 1.1) gives
  the improved upper bound a(4) <= 60", credited to Eric W. Weisstein, Nov 09 2026.

**What this establishes:** the preprint exists, claims ≤ 60, and its v1 claimed 61. **What it
does not establish:** that ≤ 60 is correct. It is unrefereed; the survey's own revision of
April 2026 predates it and therefore still prints 62; and no independent DRAT check of its
~3·10⁵ refutations has been done in this repo. The campaign's rule stands: cite it as a claim,
never as the state of the art.

## 4. What was NOT verified

- **"R₄(3) = 51 is conjectured."** The survey does *not* say this; its wording is "the most
  studied and intriguing open case". The conjecture framing appears in the abstract of
  Xu–Radziszowski, *On some open questions for Ramsey and Folkman numbers*, Problem Books in
  Mathematics, Springer (2016) 43–62, Zbl [1352.05125](https://zbmath.org/6664869) — but
  zbMATH withholds that summary, so the exact wording was not read. Until it is, the record
  should say *"the survey calls it the most studied open case; a 2016 survey chapter discusses
  R₄(3) = 51 as a conjecture"*, not "experts conjecture 51".
- **The ≤ 60 claim's internals** (the Lean reduction, the two-solver refutations): not read, not
  checked, not reproduced. Out of scope here.
- **No literature search for 2026 work on 51 itself** beyond the above. A triangle-free
  4-colouring of K₅₁ would not appear as a preprint title about upper bounds.

## 6. Database checks (added later the same day)

### 6.1 Erdős problem 183 is not about 51

OEIS A003323 links to Erdős problem 183, so it looked like a possible source for the
"R₄(3) = 51 is conjectured" framing. It is not. The page (`https://www.erdosproblems.com/183`,
read 2026-11-23, last edited 01 Sep 2026) asks for `lim_{k→∞} R(3;k)^{1/k}` — the *growth*, not
the fourth value. It offers $250, records the Xu–Xie–Chen bound `R(3;k) ≤ (e − 1/6)k! + 1`, and
states that the limit is infinite, proved by an OpenAI internal model showing
`R(3;k) ≥ k^{(1/3−o(1))k}`. None of that bears on `R₄(3)`. The conjecture framing therefore still
has no read primary source; the record should keep saying so.

Incidentally the same page confirms the upper-bound lineage we use for `R_5(3)` etc.: the
pigeonhole recursion `R(3;k) ≤ 2 + k(R(3;k−1) − 1)`.

### 6.2 The Clebsch graph in the vertex-transitive census

The record states that all six colour classes of the two K₁₆ templates are the Clebsch graph
`srg(16,5,0,2)`. The object itself is confirmed present, and unique in its class, by the
Royle–Holt census (Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.4010122, version 1.0.0, 2020; J. Symbolic Comput.
101, 51–60; CC BY 4.0; accessed through `graph-catalogs-query` on 2026-11-23):

```
graphcat.py count  alltrans16 "triangles=0"              -> 38 of 286
graphcat.py count  alltrans16 "triangles=0" --by maxdeg   -> maxdeg 2:3, 3:6, 4:12, 5:9, 6:4, 7:1, 8:1
graphcat.py graphs alltrans16 "triangles=0" "regular=yes" "mindeg=5" "maxdeg=5"
                                                          -> 9 graphs, exactly one with srg=(16,5,0,2)
```

The single `srg(16,5,0,2)` is `alltrans16_k=05 #9`, whose graph6 string is

    OsaBA`GP@`dIHWEcas_]O

So among
**vertex-transitive** graphs on 16 vertices the Clebsch graph is the only triangle-free 5-regular
one with those parameters. That does not by itself prove the templates' colour classes are
Clebsch — that is the campaign's own computation — but it does pin the object the record names.

### 6.3 What the graph catalogs cannot reach

McKay's `ramsey` collection stops at **n = 42** and holds Ramsey-(3,t)/(s,t) critical graphs
("no clique of size s and no independent set of size t"), not 4-colourings; its `r35_13.g6` is a
13-vertex R(3,5) graph. The Royle–Holt census covers **n = 10–47**. Neither reaches K₅₀ or K₅₁, so
**no catalogue can decide anything about the open case**. Two consequences worth writing down:

- the census *does* cover n = 45 and 46, where the record excludes full-circulant colour classes.
  A cross-check against all vertex-transitive graphs (not only circulants) is possible in
  principle, but `alltrans45` is 46.8 MB and `alltrans46` is 4.4 GB, both above the tool's 25 MB
  default limit; the second is out of the question here. Not run.
- at n = 15, 16 the census is complete and cheap, which is what 6.2 uses.

### 6.4 HOPS: not used, and why

Nothing in this campaign asks whether one structural graph parameter bounds another. The objects
are K₅₀/K₅₁ and their colour classes, where every parameter of interest (treewidth, clique-width,
twin-width) is at its maximum and therefore vacuous. HOPS was loaded and not queried; recording an
inert answer would be worse than recording none.
