## 1. The claim, exactly

**Theorem 1 (coverage skeleton, verified).** Let `X` be a triangle-free
4-colouring of `K_58`, let `r` have colour-degree profile `(15,14,14,14)` with
arms `A = N_0(r)`, `B_i = N_i(r)` (`|A| = 15`, `|B_i| = 14`), let
`s_c = #{u in N_c(r) : d_c(u) >= 15}`, and let `w = 15`. Then `H(r) >= 15`
implies that the frozen cover's events hold for one of the two plans:

| plan | template of `A` | thresholds `t` | events to refute | `sum_c (t_c-1)` | cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `T---` | `T15` | `(15,1,1,1)` | `s_0 >= 15`; `s_1 >= 1` (arms 2,3 by colour symmetry) | 14 | 308 |
| `U---` | `U15` | `(3,5,5,5)` | `s_0 >= 3`; `s_1 >= 5` (arms 2,3 by colour symmetry) | 14 | 73 |

*Proof.* `H(r) = s_0+s_1+s_2+s_3` with `s_0 <= 15`, `s_i <= 14`. In either plan,
if no event fires then `s_c <= t_c - 1` for every `c`, so
`H(r) <= sum_c (t_c-1) = 14 < 15 = w`. Contrapositive gives the table. `A` is
`N_0(r)`, so it carries no colour-0 edge and its internal colouring is a good
3-colouring of `K_15`; by the classification of good 3-colourings of `K_15`
(§5, input I did not re-derive) it is `U15` or `T15`, which is what selects the
plan. ∎

`n = 58` enters only through `sum_c d_c(v) = 57`: the profile `(15,14,14,14)` is
a partition of `57`, and `w = 15` is the smallest ticket mass any vertex of a
`K_58` colouring can have (four colour-degrees `<= 14` sum to at most 56). The
budget `w - 1 = 14` is therefore the smallest the method ever has.

**Corollary (what the swarm must supply).** Refuting every event in the `T---`
row and every event in the `U---` row gives `H(r) <= 14` at every
`(15,14,14,14)` vertex, i.e. W2. Refuting one row alone gives nothing: the two
rows are the two possible templates of `A`.

## 3. The threshold vector is a free knob, and the cover sets it to the weakest

Any `t` with `sum_c (t_c - 1) <= 14` is a valid plan, and
`{s_c >= t}` *shrinks* as `t` grows, so a larger threshold is a strictly
**easier** statement to refute. `plan_surface.py` enumerates all 90 admissible
plans for this profile and counts the cases each would emit (the count
reproduces the ledger's 308 and 73, which validates the accounting):

| kind of `A` | `t` | cases | `sum(t-1)` |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `T` | `(15,1,1,1)` (ledger) | **308** | 14 |
| `T` | `(12,2,2,2)` | 313 | 14 |
| `T` | `(9,3,3,3)` | 313 | 14 |
| `T` | `(6,4,4,4)` | 313 | 14 |
| `T` | `(3,5,5,5)` | 322 | 14 |
| `U` | every admissible `t`, including `(15,1,1,1)`, `(3,5,5,5)`, `(6,4,4,4)` | **73** | — |

Two facts follow.

* For the `U` template the case count is **threshold-independent** (73 whatever
  `t` is). Reason: the three 14-arms have kind `-`, no template is pinned on
  them, so there is no automorphism group to quotient and the arm group's case
  count does not depend on how many senders are demanded. The cover's `U` plan
  is cost-neutral in `t`; it is not forced.
* For the `T` template, `+5` cases buys "`>= 4` senders in each 14-arm" instead
  of "`>= 1` sender", and `+14` cases buys "`>= 5`". The ledger's choice `t_0=15,
  t_i=1` maximises the 0-arm statement and therefore pays for it with the
  weakest possible arm statement — the one that has to rule out a *single*
  sender in a 14-arm, which by `.ultramath/smwqullhx/m8/lemmas.md` R2 is exactly
  the situation the K_50 colourings realise.

**Consequence for the swarm.** The plan `t = (3,5,5,5)` is admissible for *both*
templates (322 + 73 = 395 cases instead of 381, i.e. 14 more). Under it the two
rows ask for the *same* two statements, so one structural argument could kill
all 395 cases, instead of two different arguments for 308 and 73. Conversely, if
"no sender in a 14-arm" turns out to be the obstruction, `t=(6,4,4,4)` weakens
the 0-arm requirement from "all 15" to "`>= 6`" for 5 extra cases. The 381 is a
choice, not a constraint.

## 4. Two calibrations that bound what the structural routes can reach

### 4.1 The Family-A arm count has already lost at n = 50 (machine-checked)

Suppose all 15 of `A` send. Then `d_0(a) = 1 + sum_{i=1..3} |N_0(a) cap B_i|`
with `d_0(a) in {15,16}`, and for **every** vertex `v` and arm `B_i`

  `|N_0(v) cap B_i| <= 5`   (two vertices of the intersection are neither
  0-adjacent nor 1-adjacent, so it is 2-coloured with no monochromatic triangle,
  and `R(3,3) = 6`)

hence `|N_0(a) cap B_i| in [4,5]`, `e_0(A,B_i) in [60,75]`, and
`e_0(A,W) = sum_a (d_0(a)-1) in [210,225]`, `W = B_1 u B_2 u B_3`.

`family_a_calibration.py` measures the same quantities in the three verified
`K_50` colourings at every vertex owning a 15-arm all of whose 15 members send
(35, 32 and 29 such vertices, 96 in total). Over those 96:

* `e_c(A, other arms)` ranges over **`[210, 221]`** — and `210` is exactly the
  *minimum* the `n = 58` hypothesis permits (`sum_a (d_0(a)-1)` with all
  `d_0(a) = 15`);
* the per-arm split is **always** of the form `(x, 75, 75)` with `x` in
  `[60,71]`, i.e. two of the three arms sit at the `15*5 = 75` ceiling exactly;
* `|N_c(a) cap N_i(r)|` attains 5 (the `R(3,3)=6` bound), as it must for the
  ceiling to be reached.

So the range the `n = 58` hypothesis needs — `[60,75]` per arm, `[210,225]` in
total — is not merely overlapped by the `n = 50` data, it is realised there,
including the total `210` and the per-arm ceiling `75`. A counting argument
whose only inputs are the arm exports `e_0(A,B_i)` and the cap 5 cannot separate
`n = 58` from `n = 50`; it needs an input that is not of that form (the arms'
internal structure, or the sender-free hypothesis on all three arms at once).

### 4.2 Demanding more senders does not make the cases close

`threshold_probe.py` runs one representative `(T---, arm 1)` case at thresholds
1, 4 and 6 (90 s / 1e9 conflicts each, Kissat 4.0.4, `tables=False`): all three
return `OPEN`, i.e. no closure, and the clause growth is tiny (`extra_clauses`
10361 -> 10414 -> 10438). So the wall is not an artefact of the cover asking for
too little: raising the demand does not move the solver.

Dropping the threshold altogether (`core_probe.py`, i.e. "does the pinned
two-template structure extend to a `K_58` colouring at all") is also undecided
at 3e5 conflicts in ~35 s for both the arm-0 and the arm-1 pinned core. A `SAT`
there would have been a triangle-free 4-colouring of `K_58`, hence
`R_4(3) >= 59` and a refutation of the whole campaign; I did not get one, and
that is not evidence of anything (a budget cut-off is not a proof).

## Appendix B. Why the wall sits on 14-vertex arms

Every tool that the classification buys (`m8/lemmas.md` L5, L6, L7: penetration
and the Hall form) needs a **15- or 16-vertex** set with no colour-`c` edge
inside, because `R(3,3,3) = 17` is what makes a 17-set carry a monochromatic
triangle. The three non-critical arms of a `(15,14,14,14)` vertex have **14**
vertices, one short of that threshold, so a "no sender in this arm" statement
about them gets nothing from penetration. Concretely, the one place the
machinery still bites is an arm together with one more vertex: if `x` has
`x ∉ B_1` and no colour-1 edge into `B_1`, then `S = B_1 u {x}` is a 15-set with
no colour-1 edge inside, so by L6 the set of vertices outside `S` with no
colour-1 edge into `S` is a colour-1 clique and has at most 2 elements. That is
a real constraint on the wall configuration, and it is far from a contradiction.

This is the structural reason the cover's arm statements are so weak (a single
sender in a 14-arm) and the reason §4.1 finds the counting exhausted: at 14
vertices the colour-`c` degree of an arm vertex has room in `[9,14]` with no
forcing, whereas at 15 it would be forced to 15 or 16 and the arm would be a
template.
