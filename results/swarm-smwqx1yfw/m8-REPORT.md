# Family A of the `(15,14,14,14)` wall: two new lemmas, and a machine-checked proof that no counting argument can close it

Member m8, swarm `smwqx1yfw`, direction d1. Scripts and data in this directory.
**First line, as required: this report does *not* kill Family A or Family B.** It
contains two new lemmas that are true of every good 4-colouring (one of them
`n = 58`-specific through the arm size 14), and a machine-checked **negative**
result: the complete list of counting constraints available at a
`(15,14,14,14)`-vertex — L1 (`R(3,3)=6` support bounds), arm independence, the
degree sum 57, the caps `9 <= d <= 16`, arm-profile rigidity L3/L3', both
Family-A hypotheses, and the new saturation lemma — is jointly satisfiable, *in
integers* (explicit certificate below). So Family A cannot be closed by any
argument of that form; the missing step must be a constraint that correlates two
*different* vertex neighbourhoods (one is stated in §4). Stated plainly: my
attempt at direction d1 failed to prove Family A; what follows is what I can
prove and what I can rule out.

## 0. The three statements that are actually proved here

* **Theorem A (arm-set saturation).** In a good 4-colouring of `K_n`, if `X` is
  an `m`-set with no colour-`c` edge inside and `Z = {y notin X : y has no
  colour-`c` edge into `X`}`, then `|Z| = 0` (`m=16`), `|Z| <= 2` (`m=15`),
  `|Z| <= 5` (`m=14`). Corollary: at a `(15,14,14,14)`-vertex whose 15-arm is
  fully critical, `E_f(S,A_f) >= 10` for `f = 1,2,3` — a statement with no
  calibration point, since no verified colouring has a 14-arm together with a
  fully critical 15-arm.
* **Theorem B (three-vertex correlation).** Same hypotheses: for `y in A_i` and
  `f notin {0,i}`, `sum_{z in N_f(y) cap T} |N_0(z) cap S| >= 4*|N_0(y) cap S|`
  (`>= 3*` for `f = i`).
* **Theorem C (no counting proof).** The set of constraints {L1 for all pairs,
  arm independence, `sum_f d_f(x) = 57`, `9 <= d_f(x) <= 16`, part consistency,
  L3, L3', `d_0(u) >= 15` on the 15-arm, `d_i(x) <= 14` on the arms, `2e_f(S) = 70`,
  Theorem A's three cardinality bounds, `E_f(S,A_f) >= 10`} has an integer
  solution (`milp_zeta.py`; the point is in `familyA_int_solution.json` modulo
  the indicator variables). Hence no combination of these inequalities proves
  Family A.

None of the three kills a case of the frozen cover.

## 2. The tools, and one exact cancellation that explains the difficulty

Setting: a good (no monochromatic triangle) 4-colouring of `K_58`, a vertex `r`
with profile `(15,14,14,14)`, `S = N_0(r)` (15 vertices), `A_i = N_i(r)`
(14 vertices each, `i = 1,2,3`), `T = A_1 u A_2 u A_3` (42 vertices).
`m_f(x,Q) = |N_f(x) cap Q|`, `d_f(x) = m_f(x,.)`.

**L1** (classical, from `R(3,3) = 6`): for any `x` and `f != c`,
`|N_f(x) cap N_c(v)| <= 5`. Proof: two vertices of the intersection span an
edge of colour `!= f` and `!= c`, so the intersection is 2-coloured with no
monochromatic triangle. [checked: V2]

**L3 / L3'** (arm-profile rigidity): if `d_c(v) in {15,16}` then every
`u in N_c(v)` has `5,5,5` (`d = 16`) or `4,5,5` (`d = 15`) neighbours of the
other three colours *inside* `N_c(v)`. Two instances are used below:

* `v = r`, `c = 0`: each `u in S` has `(m_1,m_2,m_3)(u,S)` a permutation of
  `(4,5,5)`, so `S` is a good 3-colouring of `K_15` in which every vertex has
  exactly one colour of degree 4. [checked: V3]
* `v = u in S`, `c = 0`: `(m_0(u,A_1), m_0(u,A_2), m_0(u,A_3))` is a
  permutation of `(5,5,4)` if `d_0(u) = 15` and `(5,5,5)` if `d_0(u) = 16`.
  [checked: V3']

Both are consequences of L1 alone; the classification of good 3-colourings of
`K_15`/`K_16` (`artifacts/k3col_classification_m4.md`) refines but is not needed
for them. What it *is* needed for: `e_f(S) = 35` for each `f = 1,2,3`, since
each colour class of a good 3-colouring of `K_15` is `G15` with five vertices of
degree 4 (`Counter({(4,5,5): 15})` on both templates; `e = (5*4+10*5)/2 = 35`).

**The cancellation.** Count the non-0 edges between `S` and `T` two ways. On the
`S` side, `u in S` sends exactly `43 - d_0(u) in {27,28}` non-0 edges into `T`
(it has 42 edges into `T` altogether, of which `d_0(u)-1 in {14,15}` are
colour 0, because `N_0(u) cap S = {}` and `N_0(u) cap {r} = {r}`). On the `A` side, `x in A_i` has 15 edges into `S`. Splitting both
counts into "same-colour" `sigma = sum_f E_f(S,A_f)` and "cross"
`kappa = sum_{f != i, f,i >= 1} E_f(S,A_i)` gives

```
kappa + sigma = 630 - E_0(S,T) = 420 - n16 ,   n16 = #{u in S : d_0(u) = 16}
u-side :  kappa <= sum_u (43 - d_0(u))  = 420 - n16        (L1: 6 terms <= 5)
A-side :  kappa <= 6 * 5 * 14           = 420              (L1: m_f(x,S) <= 5)
```
so the two constraints read `sigma >= 0` and `sigma >= -n16`: both trivial. The
bounds are *complementary* — the same `420` on both sides, produced by `5*(6*14)`
and by `15*28` — and the system has no slack but no contradiction. That is the
arithmetic reason the wall resists counting, and it is why the LP of §5 is
feasible.

## 3. New lemma: arm-set saturation (and it needs an arm of size 14)

> **Lemma A.** In a good 4-colouring of `K_n` let `X` be an `m`-set carrying no
> colour-`c` edge and `Z = {y notin X : y has no colour-`c` edge into `X`}`.
> Then `|Z| = 0` for `m = 16`, `|Z| <= 2` for `m = 15`, and `|Z| <= 5` for `m = 14`.

*Proof.* `m = 16`: `X u {y}` is a 17-set with no `c`-edge, so it is 3-coloured
and contains a monochromatic triangle (`R(3,3,3) = 17`). `m = 15`: any two
`y, y' in Z` would make `X u {y,y'}` a 17-set with no `c`-edge, so `Z` is a
`c`-clique and has at most 2 vertices. `m = 14`: three pairwise non-`c`-adjacent
vertices of `Z` would give a 17-set with no `c`-edge, so the non-`c`-graph on
`Z` is triangle-free; the `c`-graph on `Z` is triangle-free because the
colouring is good; a 2-colouring of `K_|Z|` with no monochromatic triangle has
`|Z| <= R(3,3) - 1 = 5`. QED

The `m = 14` case is exactly where `n = 58` enters: the arms have size
`14 = 17 - 3`. (At `n = 50` the arms have size 10–12, and the lemma's `m = 14`
case is vacuous there; the artefacts' 14-arms — every vertex has some colour
class of size 14 — satisfy `|Z| <= 2` with room to spare.) [checked: V5,
arm_set_lemma.py; max `|Z|` observed: 0 for m=16, 2 for m=15, 2 for m=14]

> **Corollary A'.** At a `(15,14,14,14)`-vertex `r` whose 15-arm `S` is entirely
> 0-critical: `E_f(S,A_f) >= 10` for `f = 1,2,3`.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma A with `X = A_f` (`|X| = 14`, no `f`-edges inside since
`A_f = N_f(r)`): `|Z| <= 5`. A vertex `u in S` has no `f`-edge into `A_f`
exactly when `u in Z`, so at least 10 of the 15 vertices of `S` have such an
edge. QED

(This cannot be calibrated: no verified colouring has a 14-arm together with a
fully critical 15-arm, since all artefacts have `n <= 50` and there the arms are
at most 12. That is a genuine `n = 58`-only statement, and it is the only one I
have.)

## 4. New lemma: a second-order (three-vertex) inequality

> **Lemma B.** Hypotheses of Corollary A'. For `y in A_i`, `B = N_0(y) cap S`,
> `f notin {0,i}`: `sum_{z in N_f(y) cap T} m_0(z,S) >= 4 |B|`;
> for `f = i`: `sum_{z in N_i(y) cap T} m_0(z,S) >= 3 |B|`.

*Proof.* For `u in B`, `colour(yu) = 0`, so `y in N_0(u)`; as `d_0(u) >= 15`,
L3 at `u` gives `|N_f(y) cap N_0(u)| in {4,5}` for each `f >= 1`. Summing over
`u in B` and swapping the order of summation,
`4|B| <= sum_{u in B} |N_f(y) cap N_0(u)| = sum_{z in N_f(y)} |B cap N_0(z)|`.
Now `|B cap N_0(z)| = 0` for `z in S` (`S` is 0-independent), `= |B|` for `z = r`
(and `r in N_f(y)` iff `f = i`), and `<= m_0(z,S)` for `z in T`. QED

Lemma B is *not* implied by any aggregate count: it says the number of 0-edges
from `T` into `S` is positively correlated with the `f`-edges into an arm. It
holds on every artefact and is tight at `n = 48` (`min(lhs-rhs) = 0`,
`min ratio 1.00`) and slack at `n = 49,50` (ratio 1.12–1.18)
[`second_order.py`]. I did not find a way to combine it with the degree budget;
it is recorded because it is the *shape* the missing step must have.

## 5. The no-go theorem: the counting constraints are integrally satisfiable

I encoded every constraint of §2–§3 (L1 for all pairs, arm independence
`m_0(x,S) = 0 (x in S)`, `m_i(x,A_i) = 0 (x in A_i)`, `sum_f d_f(x) = 57`,
`9 <= d_f(x) <= 16`, part-consistency `sum_{x in P} m_f(x,Q) = sum_{y in Q} m_f(y,P)`,
L3 and L3', both Family-A hypotheses `d_0(u) >= 15 (u in S)`,
`d_i(x) <= 14 (x in A_i)`, Corollary A' `E_f(S,A_f) >= 4`, and — using the
classification — `2 e_f(S) = 70`) as an LP/MILP over the 1160 variables
`m_f(x,Q)`, `x in V`, `Q` a part, `f` a colour.

```
python3 lpdiag2.py :  all 15 flag combinations  -> FEASIBLE
python3 milp_run.py:  MILP with integrality      -> OPTIMAL (feasible)
python3 milp_cert.py: an explicit integer point  -> familyA_int_solution.json
python3 milp_zeta.py: the same MILP plus ALL the sharp 17-set bounds of Lemma A
                      in indicator form
                        #{y in T : m_0(y,S) = 0} <= 2
                        #{y in T \ A_i : m_i(y,A_i) = 0} <= 5   (i = 1,2,3)
                      -> OPTIMAL (feasible), 1388 vars, 1898 rows
```

So even the *sharp* 17-set consequences, which are the strongest global
constraints the `R(3,3,3) = 17` tool yields, do not contradict the counts.
(Recording the near miss: the first run of `milp_zeta.py` returned INFEASIBLE,
but that was my encoding error — the indicator sums wrongly included the
vertices of `A_i` itself, which are excluded from `Z_i` by definition; after the
fix it is feasible. The script now contains the correct version.)

Sample of the integer certificate (`milp_cert.py` output): `r` has
`(15,14,14,14)`; the 15 vertices of `S` have 0-degrees
`16,15,15,...,15,16`; their arm-partitions are `(5,5,4)`/`(5,5,5)` as required;
`E_f(S,A_i)` matrix (rows `A_1,A_2,A_3`, columns `f=0..3`)

```
[[65 44 43 40]
 [72 60 36 43]
 [75 50 45 57]]      E_0(S,T) = 212,  E_1(S,A_1)+E_2(S,A_2)+E_3(S,A_3) = 137
```

Consequence, stated carefully: **no proof of Family A can consist of
inequalities in the numbers `m_f(x,Q)`.** Since the LP relaxes integrality and
the MILP does not, and every listed constraint is proved in §2–§3, any such
proof is impossible. The certificate is not a colouring (the variables are
aggregates, so it cannot be realised — and need not be); what it shows is that
the pairwise/aggregate layer of the problem is *empty of information*.

This is a real statement about where the difficulty is: it does not say Family A
is true, and it does not say Family A is false. It says that a proof must use a
constraint that sees at least three vertices at once, i.e. precisely something
like Lemma B, and that any future claim of the form "at a `(15,14,14,14)`-vertex
the following counting inequality contradicts L1 / the degree budget" is false.
It also independently confirms (and extends from a single vertex to the whole
Family-A structure) the qualitative claim of `.ultramath/smwqullhx/m2/NOTE.md`
§2 that no local contradiction exists.

## 6. Where `n = 58` is used — and what is not verified

`n = 58` is used in three places, and none of them is sufficient:

1. `sum_c d_c(v) = 57`, hence `9 <= d_c(v) <= 16` and `d_0(u) in {15,16}` on a
   fully critical 15-arm (§2).
2. The three non-critical arms have size **14**, exactly `R(3,3,3) - 3 = 17 - 3`,
   which is what makes Lemma A/Carollary A' non-vacuous (§3).
3. `15 + 3*14 = 57` makes the non-0-edge count in §2 exactly self-dual.

Not verified / not done:
* I did not prove or refute Family A or Family B, and no ledger case is closed;
  no DRAT certificate of any kind was produced.
* Calibration for "no fully critical 14-arm" claims (`fully_critical_arms.py`):
  a *fully c-critical* arm (every member has `d_c >= 15`) is not a rare
  phenomenon — `n48_z4z4_f0.col` has 48 of them of size 15 (all members
  `d_c = 15`), `k49_from_n48_m5.col` has 36 (including **one of size 12 whose 12
  members all have 16-vertex c-arms**, i.e. twelve `K_16` templates through one
  vertex), and `k50_z4z4_m6.col` has 35. No verified colouring has a fully
  critical arm of size 13 or 14, so the `n = 58` regime is again without a
  calibration point — but "few vertices of a critical arm are critical" is
  decisively false (35–48 examples per colouring), and any proposed lemma of
  that shape dies here.
* Lemma A's `m = 14` bound 5 is not known to be sharp inside a `K_58` colouring;
  in the artefacts the observed value is 2.
* Lemma B is verified on the artefacts only, not used to derive anything.
* `familyA_lp.py` (an early version of the LP, deleted) had the bug that the
  entries fixed by the arm structure were left out of the linear forms; the
  authoritative scripts are `lpdiag2.py`, `milp_run.py`, `milp_cert.py`,
  `milp_zeta.py`.
* The MILP optimality is HiGHS' word, not a certificate; it is used only for a
  *negative* claim (feasibility), where a wrong answer would only weaken my
  claim, not strengthen it.
