# Closing the gap-four rows of K58

**Scope.** The K58 campaign for $R(3,3,3,3)$, on the frozen cover with 113,179 rows and
31,751 open original rows. One homogeneous group in that cover is now fully refuted: the
**149 `UUU-` gap-four rows**, every one with a `drat-trim`-verified proof, for about
**2.65 CPU-hours of work including replays**. All three charge levels of the profile
$(15,15,15,12)$ are cheap to refute, so the profile's 20,166 open rows are a bulk-compute
job — about a day on twelve cores. The six small profiles (11,585 rows) are untouched and
remain the reason $R_4(3)$ stays in $[51,62]$.

This report states what is proved, what is measured, what is only extrapolated, and what
is not claimed. Author of record for every number below: the runs in `.scratch/gap4/` and
`results/gap4-2026-11-25/`, reproducible from `tools/gap4/`.

## 2. What a "row" is, and the statement that closes it

A row is $(\text{family},\text{placement},\text{charge})$: a root profile (here
$(15,15,15,12)$, a root $r$ with $|N_c(r)| = d_c$), template choices on the arms, a marked
position and placement, and a charge — the number of critical senders the campaign's cover
requires. Its CNF, as the campaign's encoder emits it, is

$$\text{host}(58) + \text{units}(\text{family},\text{placement}) + \text{dense threshold}.$$

The bundle's "pilot" is the same thing with the dense tables and total-critical dropped:
`host(58) + units + the charge-11 counters`, 171,455 clauses. Both were taken from the
bundle and verified to rebuild byte-for-byte (pilot sha256 `85aea7fc…`).

**The strong statement.** The **frozen units alone** are already contradictory. Filter the
pilot to clauses whose literals are all $\le 6612$ (i.e. drop everything the counters added)
and there are **135,498 clauses**, sha256
`4b4be140fae38664019ac35a802b48221334ac6913de0a7f3355504aab058179`, which is UNSAT:

* kissat: `s UNSATISFIABLE`, in 17–44 s over several independent runs;
* DRAT: 47,666,743 bytes, `drat-trim` prints **`s VERIFIED`** (5,267 of 135,498 clauses in
  core, 20,048,679 resolution steps);
* containment checked directly: **all 135,498 clauses appear in the row's certified CNF from
  `tools/pb/balance.py --dense`, zero missing.**

Therefore a colouring satisfying the row is impossible, irrespective of the charge. The
root-and-marked-neighbourhood geometry of these rows is **locally impossible**. That is not
a case split that happens to die; the geometry cannot be realised in any triangle-free
4-colouring of $K_{58}$.

## 3. What the other candidates cost

I used to describe this as "one elementary constraint closes the row". Two swarm members
independently overturned that, and the numbers bear them out. Measured on the same rows:

| formula | clauses | time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| frozen units alone | 135,498 | 17–44 s |
| bare pilot | 171,455 | 41 s |
| row's own certified CNF, no additions | 196,994 | 44 s |
| pilot + family B | 188,405 | ~50 s |
| row CNF + families A + B | 413,249 | ~50 s |
| pilot + A + B + C | 355,565 | 2–3 min |

Adding implied constraints is **also harmless**, because every model of a pilot is a
triangle-free 4-colouring of $K_{58}$ — so A, B and C hold in every model of every gap-four
pilot, and hence **adding them changes no verdict**. They are entailed by the object, not
by the case. In this light the cheapest objects are the ones with the fewest clauses, which
are also the ones that need no extra lemmas at all: the units.

The Lean soundness chain that carries A, B, C is nevertheless part of the record, because
these are true and reusable statements about every triangle-free 4-colouring:

* `familyB`: if $\chi(a,r)=0$ and $d_0(a)\ge 15$ then $|N_0(a)\cap A_c|\ge 4$ for each
  other arm $c$ of $r$;
* `five_bound` and `dual_five_bound`: $|N_0(a)\cap A_c|\le 5$ for each such $c$ (and its
  dual at an arm vertex), since the set is 2-coloured and $R(3,3)=6$;
* `familyC`: $|N_0(y)\cap A_0|\le 12$ at an arm vertex with in-arm colour-0 degree $\ge 4$;
* `card_nb_le_sixteen`: every colour class has at most 16 vertices;
* `exact_pattern`: A + B + the flag already pin the pattern at each critical vertex to
  $(5,5,5)$ or a permutation of $(4,5,5)$, so **no single-vertex sharpening exists**.

`lean/K58/GapFamilies.lean` compiles in ~2.7 s with no `sorry`, and each theorem depends only
on `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. The two classical inputs are explicit
hypotheses, both of which the repository's Lean core also proves separately elsewhere.

## 4. The tier structure, measured

`balance.py --dense` on sample ids of every charge level, all bare:

| profile | charge | clauses | verdict | time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| `(15,15,15,12)` | 11 | 196,994 | UNSAT | 44 s |
| `(15,15,15,12)` | 9 | 199,526 | UNSAT | 52 s |
| `(15,15,15,12)` | 3 | 196,946 | UNSAT | 39 s |

So the cover's open rows are not one homogeneous wall. They split by the obligation the row
imposes:

- charges 11, 9 and 3 in $(15,15,15,12)$ (20,166 rows) fall in under a minute;
- charges 1–3 in $(15,15,14,13)$ (7,732 rows) and in the other five small profiles do not
  fall at all inside this budget;
- a charge-10 row of $(16,15,15,11)$, whose profile has *no* open rows left in the ledger,
  closes in **2.26 s**, and I reproduced its CNF hash byte-for-byte.

Difficulty tracks **the charge and the total critical mass**, not the template or the marked
set. The gap-four case requires eleven senders of fifteen arm vertices and a total critical
mass of 33; the wall cases require one to three senders and mass 3–5. That is why the
campaign's earlier "row-by-row refutation never closes anything" measurements were both
correct and misleading: they were taken at the wall, where the row imposes almost nothing,
and extrapolated to the whole cover.

## 5. The wall, measured three ways

For one open row of `(15,15,14,13)` (`00032f34…`, arm 1, charge 1):

| object | clauses | verdict | budget |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| row CNF + A + B | 371,324 | UNKNOWN | 1200 s |
| row CNF, bare | 173,594 | UNKNOWN | 1200 s |
| units alone | 135,399 | **UNKNOWN** | 900 s |

Same object, completely different behaviour between profiles: in `(15,15,15,12)` the units
fall in 17–44 s, in `(15,15,14,13)` they do not fall in 15 minutes. The small profiles'
rows are not killed by their root-and-marked geometry — only, perhaps, by their charge.
That is genuinely new evidence about the nature of the wall, not a restatement of it, and
it points the next attack at the charge rather than at the units.

## 6. Swarm by-products, each independently reproduced

These came from the second Ultramath run and were checked by me, not just read.

* **Interface model.** With all 503 units, the arm templates, all triangles through $A_0$
  and the degree caps, the interface relaxation still admits **eleven critical arm
  vertices**, even with the campaign's support tables. So no argument confined to $A_0$ can
  force "at most ten": the interface admits eleven, and counting incidences bounds nothing
  (the true range 44–75 per arm sits inside available room).
* **The one admissible empty pattern.** For a vertex outside $A_0$ with no colour-0
  neighbours in it, the only possible colouring of its edges to $A_0$ is the *defect map* —
  search-free by $\alpha(G_c)=5$ for each colour-$c$ graph, and exhaustive over $2^{15}$.
  At most two such vertices exist in any colouring (three would be mutually colour 0, a
  monochromatic triangle), giving $\sum_{y\in Y_a}(15-s(y))\ge 12$ at **every** critical
  vertex, at degree 15 and degree 16 alike. That kills the naive realisation of eleven
  critical vertices.
* **No K16 admissible pattern at all**, by $\alpha=(5,5,5)$ with sum $15<16$.
* **$K_{15}$ and $K_{16}$ classifications** rerun end to end: two triangle-free 3-colourings
  each, $T$ and $U$ (untwisted and twisted). `nauty-geng` over all 1,021,416 candidate
  colour classes, solutions only at 35 edges, 72 labelled, two classes, with the campaign's
  own `T.col`/`U.col` landing one in each.
* **No free transport.** All 149 `UUU-` rows are singleton isomorphism classes — as are all
  83,440 rows of the profile. One certified refutation closes one row, never a family for
  free; it is the *recipe*, not the verdict, that moves from row to row.
* **Where the marked geometry does the work**. The placement-free formula (a sub-formula of
  every gap-four row's strengthened CNF) is UNKNOWN at 15.17M conflicts, while with the 131
  placement literals the same rows decide at 1.2–1.5M. The closure is carried by the marked
  units.
* **$T$ is $U$ with colours 1 and 2 exchanged and colour 0 fixed**, so a $T$ arm's colour-0
  graph is a $U$ arm's, and the families are literally the same clauses in both kinds.

## 7. What is not claimed

* $R_4(3)$ has not moved: $[51,62]$ stands.
* The `(15,15,15,12)` profile is measured on a subset of its rows (all 149 of the gap-four
  group, one sample at charge 9 and one at charge 3). Extrapolating to all 20,166 open rows
  is arithmetic assuming the same per-row cost — not done work.
* The cover's own soundness is a separate hypothesis. Each refutation above closes only its
  row, and if the cover is incomplete the rows I closed do not imply the bound. The other
  premises of the campaign (the frozen classification, the ledger's statuses, the Lean
  semantic bridge) are untouched by this work.
* DRATs were hashed and deleted after verification, as this campaign's memory policy
  requires; proofs are regenerated on demand from the listed CNF hashes and scripts.

## 8. Reproducing it

```sh
# the units alone: drop every counter clause from the bundle's pilot
zcat .scratch/k58par/campaign/gap_four_UUU_442.cnf.gz > pilot.cnf
python3 - pilot.cnf units.cnf <<'FILTER'
import sys
with open(sys.argv[1]) as f, open(sys.argv[2], 'w') as g:
    head = f.readline().split()
    nv = int(head[2])
    clauses = []
    for line in f:
        if not line.strip():
            continue
        lits = [int(x) for x in line.split()[:-1]]
        if all(abs(x) <= 6612 for x in lits):
            clauses.append(lits)
    g.write('p cnf %d %d\n' % (nv, len(clauses)))
    g.write(''.join(' '.join(map(str, c)) + ' 0\n' for c in clauses))
print(len(clauses))          # -> 135498
FILTER
sha256sum units.cnf          # -> 4b4be140fae38664019ac35a802b48221334ac6913de0a7f3355504aab058179
kissat -q --time=900 units.cnf units.drat    # -> s UNSATISFIABLE
drat-trim units.cnf units.drat -c            # -> s VERIFIED
    clauses = []
PY

# the bulk handoff
export PYTHONPATH=<site-packages with pysat>
bash tools/gap4/run_units_bulk.sh \
     results/gap4-2026-11-25/open-row-ids-15151512.txt OUT 12
```

`run_units_bulk.sh` builds each row CNF with `tools/pb/balance.py --dense`, filters it to
`host + units`, solves, checks the DRAT, records `row, verdict, seconds, units_sha,
drat_sha`, deletes the proof after hashing, and resumes by skipping rows already in
`verdicts.tsv`. The file `results/gap4-2026-11-25/open-row-ids-15151512.txt` is the
authoritative list of all 20,166 open rows of `(15,15,15,12)`.
